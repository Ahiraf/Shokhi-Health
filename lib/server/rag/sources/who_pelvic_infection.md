## Symptoms have many causes
Pelvic pain, unusual or foul-smelling discharge, burning when urinating, pain during sex and fever can be caused by different conditions, including sexually transmitted infections. Some infections cause no symptoms, so symptoms alone cannot confirm the cause.

## Testing and treatment
Confidential testing and advice from a trained health worker are safer than choosing antibiotics from a symptom list. Some infections are treatable, and treatment should follow the clinician’s assessment. A partner may also need testing or treatment.

## Protecting health
Condoms used correctly and consistently reduce the risk of many sexually transmitted infections. Ask the health worker whether to pause sex or use condoms until testing and treatment are complete. During pregnancy, report fever, pelvic pain or unusual discharge promptly.

## Urgent care
Seek prompt care for severe pelvic pain, fever, fainting, pregnancy with concerning symptoms, or rapidly worsening illness. A person who experienced sexual violence should be offered confidential, respectful and urgent support.
