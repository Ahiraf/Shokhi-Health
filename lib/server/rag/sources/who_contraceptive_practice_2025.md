## Why this document is needed
The Selected practice recommendations (SPR), fourth edition, explains how to start, continue and manage problems with contraceptive methods after a method is considered medically appropriate. It complements the WHO Medical eligibility criteria already used by Shokhi.

## Missed or late pills
Advice depends on the pill type, how many hormonal pills were missed, where the person is in the pack, and whether unprotected sex happened. A safe digital helper should first identify the product and situation rather than give one rule for every pill. If the product or timing is unclear, the person should check the pack and speak with a pharmacist or family-planning worker.

## Emergency contraception
Emergency contraception should be used as soon as possible after unprotected sex. Depending on the method, emergency contraception can be used within 5 days. The copper-bearing IUD is a highly effective option when provided by a qualified health worker. Emergency contraception does not protect against sexually transmitted infections and should not replace a regular method.

## Person-centred choice
Counselling should respect the person's own decision and consider health history, breastfeeding, future pregnancy plans, preference for a method that needs little daily remembering, and protection from sexually transmitted infections. General information is not a prescription; a trained health worker can help choose and use a method safely.
