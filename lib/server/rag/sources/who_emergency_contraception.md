## When it may help
Emergency contraception can help prevent pregnancy after unprotected sex or when contraception may have failed, such as a broken condom or missed pills. It is intended for occasional use after an unexpected event, not as a replacement for a regular method.

## Timing and support
It works best when used as soon as possible. A trained health worker or pharmacist can help explain the available options and which one fits the person’s situation. Follow the product instructions and ask about a regular family-planning method afterward.

## What it does not do
Emergency contraception does not end an established pregnancy and does not protect against sexually transmitted infections. Condoms can reduce the risk of many infections. A person who experienced forced sex should be offered confidential, supportive and urgent care.

## When to seek care
Seek health-worker advice if pregnancy is possible, the next period is unexpectedly late, there is severe abdominal pain or unusual bleeding, or there are concerns about sexual violence. Emergency contraception does not harm future fertility.
